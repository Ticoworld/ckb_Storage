# Alternative storage and balance models

All comparisons use the same target: independently controlled 1 CKB in a fresh standard-shaped CKB L1 output. Fees and collateral remain separate. A shared claim, pre-existing ACP, smart-contract object, or payment channel can be useful, but is not counted as a fresh independent output.

| Model | Initial cost for target | Duration and offline behavior | Main resource/security trade-off | WO03 assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current CKB occupied capacity | 61 CKB locked, including 1 CKB principal; 60 additional recoverable capacity | Perpetual while the output exists; no renewal or operator | Capacity demand binds state bytes to native issuance/DAO accounting; high token price raises fiat commitment | Strong direct ownership and no per-cell expiry; expensive at high token prices |
| 61-byte/CKB conversion for new balances | 1 CKB locked for 61-byte output | Perpetual, assuming protocol protects state | Makes each CKB unit represent 61 times as many bytes; increasing affordable demand can multiply state load; requires a rule for old rights and miner income | Technically coherent fork parameter, but rights and security budget are unproven |
| Term rent | Small upfront collateral plus rent | If rent lapses, deletion or hibernation; offline owner needs grace, recovery, and an enforceable receipt | Recurring fee can match resource time; absent trustworthy costs, rent may be too low or exclude users; deletion does not preserve L1 monetary state | Solana demonstrates rent-exempt deposits and paid/collectible rent design history, but not a free perpetual state solution |
| Prepaid storage endowment | Present value of future storage service | Must fund indefinite service or define expiry | If funded from CKB, funding asset is volatile and required endowment is large when yield is low; external diversified assets introduce custody/oracle/counterparty risk | Sui's storage fund accounts for rebates and nonrefundable portions; this moves accounting rather than eliminating resource cost |
| Separately traded storage credits | Market value of the required 61-byte right plus principal | Credit must remain valid while output exists; long offline control requires transfer/recovery semantics | Supply scarcity and collateral must be explicit; credits cannot be counted both as old CKB rights and new credits | Could reveal a market price for storage, but no evidence that it reduces total cost under full legacy redemption |
| Bounded storage auction / dynamic quota | Auction price plus 1 CKB principal | State availability while admitted; auction/renewal and censorship matter | A protocol byte budget bounds growth; value is allocated by bidding and sybils/miner capture must be handled | Plausible congestion management; does not guarantee affordable access or unpriced legacy exercise |
| Account abstraction / compact output | Same global occupied-capacity rule absent protocol change | Depends on cell/output representation | Lock-argument savings affect a few bytes; global 61-byte output remains | Current-consensus optimization cannot solve the economic threshold by itself |
| Channel or shared UTXO | Can amortize state across many users | Depends on liquidity, counterparties, dispute window, data, or shared proof state | Reduces replicated state at the cost of settlement, availability, capacity or operator assumptions | Existing alternative when shared/off-chain ownership is acceptable; WO02 already stopped its pool experiment |

## Cross-chain designs and what they actually buy

**Solana.** At the pinned Agave/Solana SDK versions, account rent exemption is a balance threshold derived from account data length and a rent parameter; accounts underfunded for rent can be charged/collected by runtime logic. SIMD-0194 is marked Activated in the pinned proposal and removes floating-point threshold behavior; later SIMD-0437 (incremental rent reduction) and SIMD-0438 (safeguard against increases) are marked Idea at the cited snapshot. The model is not simply a current two-year subscription: rent-exempt capital is locked while the account exists, and lowering the required deposit is explicitly treated as a state-growth risk with a possible reversal path. This is a close analogy for refundable state collateral, not proof that a lower threshold pays for unlimited indefinite state.

**Sui.** Pinned `storage_fund.move` maintains a total object-rebate balance and a nonrefundable balance. Storage charges flow into the rebate obligation; deletion/modification rebates are paid out with an amount removed to the nonrefundable side; epoch processing also accepts storage-fund reinvestment and leftover rewards. This is a lifecycle fund and partial refund mechanism, not a guarantee that object creation has no capital cost. It depends on Sui's object/gas/epoch protocol and does not transfer directly to CKB's cell and DAO rules.

**Cardano.** The pinned Alonzo ledger source expresses minimum UTxO in terms of a protocol parameter and serialized output size; Babbage-era sources remain parameterized around min-coin calculation. This provides an adjustable native-value-per-output floor, but a parameter adjustment does not decouple monetary state from its funding token, guarantee hardware-cost accuracy, or answer legacy migration. The historical minimum-UTxO rule is a policy parameter, not evidence that lowering it is sustainable at all demand levels.

**Ethereum.** EIP-2929 reprices cold state access; EIP-3529 reduces gas refunds; EIP-4444 concerns pruning old historical chain data, and EIP-4844 introduces data blobs with bounded availability rather than expiring account balances. Gas repricing and history pruning do not make persistent account storage disappear or give a small balance a cheaper independently enforceable state slot. Historical data retention and current state availability are distinct obligations.

## IF / ELSE summary

If a protocol charges storage only once and lets the claim persist indefinitely, miners/nodes inherit an unpriced future service obligation unless endowment or other income covers it. If it charges recurrent rent, offline users need a guaranteed renewal/exit mechanism and the total rent can exceed today's refundable capital. If it fixes a lower byte conversion, an explicit cap or independent sustainable funding source must absorb induced state growth. If it creates a tradable credit, supply and legacy priority determine scarcity; the credit is not free collateral. If it prunes history, that does not erase current monetary state. If it delegates state to an operator, it changes the user's control guarantee.
